Neurosynth’s Association and Uniformity Tests as Probability Ratios#

Neurosynth [yarkoni2011] maps each term with two tests on the counts of studies activating each voxel:

  • the uniformity test (formerly “forward inference”): is the proportion of the studies of the term activating the voxel different from its average over the brain?

  • the association test (formerly “reverse inference”): are the term and the activation of the voxel independent? The map comes with the posterior probability of the term given the activation, for a uniform prior of 0.5.

Both are \(\chi^2\) tests, and both reduce to a ratio of probabilities which is 1 when there is no effect. With selected_study the uniform choice over the \(N\) studies of the neurosynth engine, every probability of a query is a count of studies divided by \(N\), so these ratios are queries of the engine.

The ratios#

Let \(T\) be the event that the selected study has the term and \(A_v\) that it activates the voxel \(v\).

Association. \(T\) and \(A_v\) are independent if and only if the lift

\[L_v = \frac{P(A_v \mid T)}{P(A_v)} = \frac{P(T \mid A_v)}{P(T)} = \frac{P(A_v \land T)}{P(A_v)\, P(T)}\]

is 1, and the \(\chi^2\) statistic of the test is \(N\) times the squared distance of the lift to 1:

\[\chi^2_\text{association} = N\, (L_v - 1)^2\, \frac{P(A_v)\, P(T)}{P(\lnot A_v)\, P(\lnot T)}.\]

The relative risk \(RR_v = P(A_v \mid T) / P(A_v \mid \lnot T)\) is also 1 under independence, and Neurosynth’s posterior for a prior of 0.5 is \(RR_v / (1 + RR_v)\).

Uniformity. The average over the \(M\) voxels of the mask of \(P(A_v \mid T)\) is itself a probability: with selected_voxel a uniform choice over the voxels and \(V\) the voxel it selects, it is \(\bar p = P(A_V \mid T)\), the probability that the selected study activates the selected voxel, given the term. The ratio

\[U_v = \frac{P(A_v \mid T)}{P(A_V \mid T)} = M\, P(V = v \mid A_V, T)\]

is 1 under uniformity, and, with \(N\, P(T)\) the number of studies of the term,

\[\chi^2_\text{uniformity} = N\, P(T)\, (U_v - 1)^2\, \frac{\bar p}{1 - \bar p}.\]

Predefined in the engines#

The neurosynth and neurostore engines define these ratios for any term, so that a query is one line, with the term as a constant (the engine then computes the rules for it only):

ans(i, j, k, uniformity) :- uniformity('working memory', i, j, k, uniformity)
ans(i, j, k, lift) :- lift('vision', i, j, k, lift)
ans(i, j, k, lift, relative_risk, uniformity) :-
    ratios('pain', i, j, k, lift, relative_risk, uniformity)

lift, relative_risk, uniformity and ratios are the quantities of the previous section, as predicates (term, voxel, ratio…), and they are listed by --list-predicates. Their definitions are the rules of the next section, with three differences: a term is in a study when its TF-IDF is above 0.001 (term_in_study), a study activates the voxels of the whole brain within 6 mm of its peaks (activated; brain_mask), and the average of the uniformity test is over the brain rather than over cortical_mask, so that the subcortical structures, the cerebellum and the hippocampus are in the map; selected_voxel is the uniform choice over the voxels of the brain. The maps of working memory take about ten seconds for the uniformity ratio and half a minute for the three ratios on the 2 mm grid. In the neurostore engine the terms are those of its TF-IDF. A probabilistic query needs the term as a constant: without one the rules cannot be rewritten for it.

Free-text queries#

The same ratios exist for a free text instead of a term, with the suffix _llm: uniformity_llm, lift_llm, relative_risk_llm and ratios_llm. The studies of the “term” are then the 5% of the studies closest to the text, by the cosine similarity of the SPECTER2 embeddings of their title and abstract to the text (see Semantic Search Over Study Abstracts):

ans(i, j, k, u) :- uniformity_llm('cortical response to noxious heat applied to the skin', i, j, k, u)

study_similarity(text, study, similarity) gives the similarity of every study with a text, and similar_study(text, study) the studies of the top 5%, which one can use in rules of one’s own. As for a term, the text is a constant of the query. Neither it nor the predicates can be computed for every text, so they are left out of solve_all. The first query embeds the abstracts (the model, about 430 MB, and the embeddings are cached; see Semantic Search Over Study Abstracts to compute them ahead of time) and needs pip install "neurolang[embeddings]".

SPECTER2 is for descriptions that do not contain the term: for a keyword, its TF-IDF, that is uniformity('pain', ...), is the better selection. On 36 Neurosynth terms described by three sentences that contain neither the term nor its variants, the map of the studies close to the text identified the term among the 36 first in about a third of the cases, against a fifth for the TF-IDF of the same sentence.

In the REPL#

Neurosynth works on the 2 mm MNI grid, a study activating the voxels within 6 mm of its peaks, and a term being in the studies with a TF-IDF above 0.001. Start the REPL of the neurosynth engine on the 2 mm grid:

python -m neurolang.utils.cli -e neurosynth -d neurolang_data -r 2 -i

and paste this block at the prompt. It computes the lift and the uniformity ratio of the term vision over the voxels of cortical_mask, shows the five voxels of the largest uniformity ratios, and opens both maps in the viewer. Each line is preceded by a % comment saying what it computes, which the REPL skips. The rules which differ from the engine’s, being over the cortex, end with _cortex, as a second rule for a predicate of the engine would add to its rules rather than replace them:

% T, term_in_study(s, t), and P(T), p_term(t, PROB), are the engine's: the TF-IDF of the term t in the study s is above 0.001
% A_v: the study s activates the voxel (i, j, k) of the cortex, within 3 voxels (6 mm) of one of its peaks
activation(i, j, k, s) :- cortical_mask(i, j, k) & peak_reported(i1, j1, k1, s) & (EUCLIDEAN(i, j, k, i1, j1, k1) <= 3)
% M: the number of voxels of the cortex
n_voxels(count(i, j, k)) :- cortical_mask(i, j, k)
% V: a uniform choice over the voxels of the cortex, each of probability 1 / M
selected_voxel_cortex(i, j, k) ^ (1 / m) :- cortical_mask(i, j, k) & n_voxels(m)
% P(A_v): the probability that the selected study activates the voxel
p_a_cortex(i, j, k, PROB) :- activation(i, j, k, s) & selected_study(s)
% P(A_v | T): the probability that a study with the term activates the voxel
p_a_given_t_cortex(t, i, j, k, PROB) :- (activation(i, j, k, s) & selected_study(s)) // (term_in_study(s, t) & selected_study(s))
% P(A_V | T): the same for the selected voxel, the average of P(A_v | T) over the cortex
p_any_voxel_given_t_cortex(t, PROB) :- (activation(i, j, k, s) & selected_voxel_cortex(i, j, k) & selected_study(s)) // (term_in_study(s, t) & selected_study(s))
% the lift P(A_v | T) / P(A_v), the relative risk P(A_v | T) / P(A_v | not T) and the uniformity ratio P(A_v | T) / P(A_V | T)
ratios_cortex(t, i, j, k, lift, relative_risk, uniformity) :- p_a_given_t_cortex(t, i, j, k, pat) & p_a_cortex(i, j, k, pa) & p_term(t, pt) & p_any_voxel_given_t_cortex(t, pbar) & (lift == pat / pa) & (relative_risk == pat * (1 - pt) / (pa - pat * pt)) & (uniformity == pat / pbar)
% sort the results by decreasing uniformity ratio
.sort uniformity:desc
% show the first 5 rows
.head 5
% the ratios of the term vision: magic sets compute the rules above for it only
ans(i, j, k, lift, uniformity) :- ratios_cortex("vision", i, j, k, lift, relative_risk, uniformity)
% open the lift and uniformity maps in the viewer
.view

The comments say what each line computes; some points need more words:

  • The term is only given in the query, ratios_cortex("vision", ...): magic sets pass the constant "vision" to ratios_cortex, then to p_term, p_a_given_t_cortex, p_any_voxel_given_t_cortex and term_in_study, so that the probabilistic rules are only computed for this term. Change it in the query to map another one; the rules stay defined.

  • activation holds the voxels within 6 mm, 3 voxels of the grid, of a peak of each study, the EUCLIDEAN distance being in voxels.

  • p_any_voxel_given_t_cortex is \(\bar p = P(A_V \mid T)\), a single probability per term: the selected study and the selected voxel are chosen independently, so it is the average over the \(M\) voxels of \(P(A_v \mid T)\).

  • ratios_cortex divides the probabilities. The relative risk is computed from them, as \(P(A_v \mid \lnot T) = (P(A_v) - P(A_v \mid T) P(T)) / (1 - P(T))\), rather than with a query conditioned on ~term_in_study(s, t), which is slower.

The voxels of the largest uniformity ratios, (74, 32, 38) and (73, 32, 36) of the grid, are at \((50, -70, 4)\) and \((48, -70, 0)\) mm in MNI space, in the right lateral occipital cortex (inferior division), around the motion area MT+ (V5): the studies of vision activate them 11 times more often than the average voxel of the cortex.

On the views, the keys V and C cycle the views and the clip planes.

Notes:

  • The mask is that of cortical_mask: the average of the uniformity test is over the cortex, whereas Neurosynth’s is over the whole brain.

  • A voxel which no study of the term activates is not in the result: its ratios are 0. The relative risk is infinite at a voxel which only the studies of the term activate.

  • The lift and the relative risk are largest at voxels which few studies activate, where they are the least stable: to rank the voxels as Neurosynth’s association test does, use the mutual information of the next section.

Ranking the voxels as Neurosynth does#

Neurosynth’s association map is not a map of the lift: it is the \(z\)-score of the \(\chi^2\) test, kept where it passes a false discovery rate of 0.01. The statistic multiplies the squared distance of the lift to 1 by \(N\, P(A_v)\, P(T) / (P(\lnot A_v)\, P(\lnot T))\), which weighs down the voxels that few studies activate. The two maps differ most for a frequent term. prefrontal is in 4,400 of the 14,371 studies, so its lift is at most \(1 / P(T) = 3.27\), and that maximum is reached at \((12, -104, 18)\) mm, in the occipital pole, which only 2 studies activate, both of them with the term. Neurosynth’s largest \(z\), 10.1, is at \((42, 38, 30)\) mm, in the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, where 247 of the 473 studies activating the voxel have the term: a lift of only 1.71.

The mutual information of \(T\) and \(A_v\) is the expectation of the logarithm of the lift over the four combinations of the term and the activation,

\[I_v = \sum_{x \in \{T, \lnot T\}} \sum_{y \in \{A_v, \lnot A_v\}} P(x, y) \log \frac{P(x, y)}{P(x)\, P(y)},\]

0 under independence, and \(2 N I_v\) is the likelihood-ratio statistic of the same independence test, which follows the same \(\chi^2\) distribution with one degree of freedom. It is a rule over the probabilities that the engine predefines, p_term, p_a and p_a_given_t, the probabilities of the four combinations being

\[P(T, A_v) = P(T)\, P(A_v \mid T), \quad P(T, \lnot A_v) = P(T) - P(T, A_v), \quad P(\lnot T, A_v) = P(A_v) - P(T, A_v), \quad P(\lnot T, \lnot A_v) = 1 - P(A_v) - P(T) + P(T, A_v).\]

The rule keeps the voxels which the term activates more often than the other studies do, \(P(A_v \mid T) > P(A_v)\), as Neurosynth’s positive map does:

mutual_information(t, i, j, k, mi) :- p_a_given_t(t, i, j, k, pat) & p_a(i, j, k, pa) & p_term(t, pt) & (pat > pa) & (mi == pt * pat * log(pat / pa) + pt * (1 - pat) * log((1 - pat) / (1 - pa)) + (pa - pt * pat) * log((pa - pt * pat) / (pa * (1 - pt))) + (1 - pa - pt + pt * pat) * log((1 - pa - pt + pt * pat) / ((1 - pa) * (1 - pt))))
ans(i, j, k, mi) :- mutual_information("prefrontal", i, j, k, mi)

It takes about 25 seconds on the 2 mm grid. To compare it with Neurosynth, take the 5% of the 235,375 voxels of brain_mask with the largest values of each map, 11,769 voxels, and measure the overlap of the two sets with the Dice coefficient, \(2 |A \cap B| / (|A| + |B|)\). The mutual information ranks the voxels as the \(z\)-score does: the \(z\) recomputed from the same counts overlaps no more with Neurosynth’s map, so what differs comes from the data, such as how the Talairach peaks are converted to MNI, rather than from the statistic. The voxels of its top 5% are activated by a median of 246 studies, against 143 for the lift’s.

A probability of association can be derived from it: comparing the model in which the term and the activation are independent with the one in which they are not, the Bayesian information criterion approximates the log of the Bayes factor by \(N I_v - \tfrac{1}{2} \log N\), so that, for a prior of 0.5,

\[P(\text{association} \mid \text{data}) \approx \frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{N}\, e^{-N I_v}},\]

which ranks the voxels as \(I_v\) does.
