Semantic Search Over Study Abstracts#

NeuroSynth links studies to terms through TF-IDF counts over a fixed vocabulary. The neurosynth engine also lets you rank studies by how close their title and abstract are to any free-text query. Studies are embedded with SPECTER2 [singh2023] (document adapter allenai/specter2), queries with its ad-hoc query adapter (allenai/specter2_adhoc_query), and the score is the cosine similarity between the two embeddings.

The feature needs extra packages:

pip install "neurolang[embeddings]"

Note

The SPECTER2 weights are stored as PyTorch .bin files, which recent versions of transformers only load with torch>=2.6. On platforms where an older torch is the latest available (e.g. Intel macOS), install adapters==1.1.1 and transformers~=4.48.3.

Titles and abstracts come from PubMed. The first query that uses the similarity downloads them (about three minutes for the 14,371 NeuroSynth studies), downloads SPECTER2 and its adapters (about 430 MB), embeds the studies (minutes on a GPU, longer on a CPU), and caches all of it under {data_dir}/neurosynth/embeddings/, the model and its tokenizer in models/. To do it ahead of time:

python -m neurolang.utils.text_embeddings -e neurosynth -d neurolang_data

After that, queries need no network: new query texts are embedded with the local copy of the model. To work offline on another machine, e.g. a compute node without internet, run this command once on a machine with internet and copy the data directory.

NCBI asks for a contact e-mail with E-utilities requests. It is optional; set it, and optionally an API key raising the rate limit from 3 to 10 requests per second, through the environment:

export NEUROLANG_ENTREZ_EMAIL=you@example.org
export NCBI_API_KEY=...

Without torch, as in the REPL in the browser, queries are embedded by a numpy version of the encoder (neurolang.utils.specter2_numpy), which gives the same embeddings to within float16 rounding, from the base model and the query adapter exported with specter2_numpy.export next to the local copies (Specter2Encoder.numpy_path); the studies must have been embedded beforehand. The page downloads both, about 310 MB, the first time a query uses query_similarity.

The engine configuration is described in Text embeddings.

Similarity of every study to a query#

query_similarity(query, s) returns the cosine similarity between the title and abstract of study s and the text query, or nan if s is not a NeuroSynth study. It is a function, so a rule gets the similarity with an equality and ranges over the studies with study. Here with the neurolang-query command line (python -m neurolang.utils.cli):

python -m neurolang.utils.cli -e neurosynth --sort sim:desc \
    "ans(s, sim) :- study(s) & \
        (sim == query_similarity('visual word form reading', s)) \
        & (sim > 0.78)"

The query text can also be a variable of a rule, as long as the rule is queried, or called, with a constant for it: magic sets pass the constant to the rule. In the neurolang-query interactive mode:

close(w, s) :- study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(w, s)) & (sim > .78)
ans(s) :- close('visual word form reading', s)

Querying close(w, s) without a constant for w fails with an UnboundVariablesError: the similarity cannot be computed without a query text.

The query text can also flow through a probabilistic rule. Here q is a variable of the conditional rule voxel_given_sim, bound by the constant of the query and passed to close:

close(q, s) :- study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s)) & (sim > .75)
voxel_given_sim(q, i, j, k, PROB) :- (peak_reported(i, j, k, s) & selected_study(s)) // (close(q, s) & selected_study(s))
ans(i, j, k, p) :- voxel_given_sim('math', i, j, k, p)

Each query text, e.g. 'math' and 'reading' in both(i, j, k, p, r) :- voxel_given_sim('math', i, j, k, p) & voxel_given_sim('reading', i, j, k, r), gets its own conditional probabilities. A constant written in a rule works as well, e.g. math_study(s) :- close('math', s) used in the condition // (math_study(s) & selected_study(s)). The query texts can also come from a stored relation with a constant joined in the query, e.g. ans(i, j, k, p) :- topic("maths", q) & voxel_given_sim(q, i, j, k, p): magic sets pass the texts of topic with "maths" to close, see below. A relation without a constant, as topic(q) in ans(q, i, j, k, p) :- topic(q) & voxel_given_sim(q, i, j, k, p), does not pass its values to close: it must bind q in the body of close itself, e.g. close(q, s) :- topic(q) & study(s) & ....

SPECTER2 similarities fall in a narrow range: for this query they average 0.71 with a standard deviation of 0.03 over NeuroSynth, and 0.78 keeps about the top 1% of the studies. Pick thresholds from the distribution rather than as absolute values: the percentile aggregation computes, for each query text, the similarity above which the top 1% of the studies lie, and close keeps them, whatever the query:

cutoff(q, percentile(sim, 99)) :- study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s))
close(q, s) :- study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s)) & cutoff(q, t) & (sim >= t)
voxel_given_sim(q, i, j, k, PROB) :- (peak_reported(i, j, k, s) & selected_study(s)) // (close(q, s) & selected_study(s))
ans(i, j, k, p) :- voxel_given_sim('math', i, j, k, p)

Each query text is embedded once and the similarities of all the studies to it are cached (the 16 most recent query texts), so each call is a lookup.

Activations within a radius of the peaks#

A study rarely reports a peak exactly at a voxel: the activation of a study can be taken as the voxels within a radius of its peaks, with the EUCLIDEAN distance bound (section 4.8 of SQUALL: Controlled English for NeuroLang describes it in SQUALL; the pairs within the distance are computed once, when the rule is defined). Combined with the percentile cutoff above, this gives the probability that a voxel is within 2 voxels of a peak of a study, given that the study is among the top 1% closest to the query text:

cutoff(q, percentile(sim, 99)) :- study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s))
close(q, s) :- study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s)) & cutoff(q, c) & (sim >= c)
near_peak(i, j, k, s) :- voxel(i, j, k) & peak_reported(i1, j1, k1, s) & (EUCLIDEAN(i, j, k, i1, j1, k1) < 2)
voxel_given_sim(q, i, j, k, PROB) :- (near_peak(i, j, k, s) & selected_study(s)) // (close(q, s) & selected_study(s))
ans(i, j, k, p) :- voxel_given_sim('math', i, j, k, p)

The radius is in voxels of the engine’s grid, so 2 is 2 times its resolution in millimetres.

Query texts from the Cognitive Atlas#

The query text can be the description of a task of the Cognitive Atlas, from cognitive_task(task_id, name, description), joined with voxel_given_sim in the query: the descriptions of the tasks named in the query are passed by magic sets to cutoff and close:

cutoff(q, percentile(sim, 95)) :- study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s))
close(q, s) :- study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s)) & cutoff(q, c) & (sim >= c)
near_peak(i, j, k, s) :- voxel(i, j, k) & peak_reported(i1, j1, k1, s) & (EUCLIDEAN(i, j, k, i1, j1, k1) < 3)
voxel_given_sim(q, i, j, k, PROB) :- (near_peak(i, j, k, s) & selected_study(s)) // (close(q, s) & selected_study(s))
ans(i, j, k, p) :- cognitive_task(_, "color-word stroop task", d) & voxel_given_sim(d, i, j, k, p)

The map is that of the studies among the top 5% closest to the description of the task. The constant "color-word stroop task" is what lets magic sets pass the description: with cognitive_task(_, _, d), every description would be a query text, and the query fails with an UnboundVariablesError (In cutoff: the variables sim, q appear only in ...). To map several tasks, name them in a relation of query texts which binds q in the bodies of cutoff and close:

task_query(d) :- cognitive_task(_, "color-word stroop task", d)
task_query(d) :- cognitive_task(_, "stop signal task", d)
cutoff(q, percentile(sim, 95)) :- task_query(q) & study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s))
close(q, s) :- task_query(q) & study(s) & (sim == query_similarity(q, s)) & cutoff(q, c) & (sim >= c)

Each description is embedded as a query text, and the similarities to the 16 most recent ones are cached: list the tasks of interest rather than every task of the Cognitive Atlas.

The term-based counterpart replaces the similarity by the TF-IDF of a term. Rather than keeping the studies whose TF-IDF passes a threshold \(\tau\), Iovene et al. [iovene2021] make the association of a term to a study a probabilistic fact, whose probability is a soft threshold of the TF-IDF \(x\),

\[\omega(x; \alpha, \tau) = \sigma(\alpha (x - \tau)) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\alpha (x - \tau)}},\]

which tends to the hard threshold \(\mathbf{1}[x > \tau]\) as \(\alpha\) grows. They report \(\alpha = 300\), with \(\tau = 0.1\) in their experiments; the mentions relation of the engine uses a hard threshold of 0.03 instead. A query-based probabilistic fact, head :: probability :- body, defines it:

term_in_study(s, t) :: 1 / (1 + exp(-300 * (w - 0.1))) :- term_in_study_tfidf(s, t, w)
near_peak(i, j, k, s) :- voxel(i, j, k) & peak_reported(i1, j1, k1, s) & (EUCLIDEAN(i, j, k, i1, j1, k1) < 2)
voxel_given_term(t, i, j, k, PROB) :- (near_peak(i, j, k, s) & selected_study(s)) // (term_in_study(s, t) & selected_study(s))
ans(i, j, k, p) :- voxel_given_term('pain', i, j, k, p)

On NeuroSynth, this query takes about 9 s: 435 of the 516 studies with a TF-IDF for ‘pain’ are above \(\tau = 0.1\), and the probabilities reach 0.025.

Forward inference from free text#

The studies closest to a query can replace the term association of the usual NeuroSynth forward-inference query. The rules below keep as many studies as are tagged with the term ‘reading’, with an aggregation nth_largest defined in Python (see the example Forward Inference From a Free-Text Query.):

from pathlib import Path
from typing import Iterable

import numpy as np
from neurolang.utils.engine_registry import build_engine

nl = build_engine("neurosynth", Path("neurolang_data"), resolution=4)

@nl.add_symbol
def nth_largest(values: Iterable, n: Iterable) -> float:
    return float(np.sort(np.asarray(values))[-int(np.asarray(n)[0])])

text_map = nl.execute_datalog_program(
    "similarity(s, v) :- study(s) & "
    "(v == query_similarity('visual word form reading', s)).\n"
    "reading_study(s) :- term_in_study_tfidf(s, 'reading', w).\n"
    "n_reading(count(s)) :- reading_study(s).\n"
    "cutoff(nth_largest(v, n)) :- similarity(s, v) & n_reading(n).\n"
    "text_study(s) :- similarity(s, v) & cutoff(c) & (v >= c).\n"
    "activation(i, j, k) :- selected_study(s) & peak_reported(i, j, k, s).\n"
    "relevant(s) :- selected_study(s) & text_study(s).\n"
    "marg(i, j, k, PROB) :- activation(i, j, k) // relevant(s).\n"
    "ans(i, j, k, p) :- marg(i, j, k, p)."
)

text_map gives, for each voxel, the probability that a study reports a peak there given that it is among the 521 studies closest to the query. On NeuroSynth v7 at 4 mm, 17 of the 20 closest studies and 207 of the 521 are tagged ‘reading’, and the map correlates with the ‘reading’ term map after smoothing both with an 8 mm Gaussian kernel, with r = 0.97 (Dice overlap of the top 5% voxels 0.84).

Other engines can use the same machinery on their own documents, from PubMed or from a table of texts, through the text_embeddings section of engines.yaml.

[iovene2021]

Iovene, V., Zanitti, G. E., & Wassermann, D. (2021). Complex Coordinate-Based Meta-Analysis with Probabilistic Programming. Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 35(1), 223–231.

[singh2023]

Singh, A., D’Arcy, M., Cohan, A., Downey, D., & Feldman, S. (2023). SciRepEval: A Multi-Format Benchmark for Scientific Document Representations. EMNLP 2023.
