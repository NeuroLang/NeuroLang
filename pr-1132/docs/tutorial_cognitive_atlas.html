Querying the Cognitive Atlas Ontology#

The neurosynth and neurostore engines load the Cognitive Atlas ontology (cogat.v2.owl of the cogat-ontology repository: about 1,800 classes, the concepts, tasks and task contrasts of the field, with their labels, definitions and relations) next to the studies, terms and peaks. Nothing is copied into tables: the ontology is read by load_ontology() and stays an ontology. Its triples are facts that rules and queries join with the data of the engine, and its axioms (a class rdfs:subClassOf another) are constraints that rewrite the queries, which is NeuroLang’s open-world reasoning (see RDF Data and Ontologies). The file is downloaded once into {data_dir}/{engine}/, and loading it takes about two seconds.

This page shows how to pull information out of it. The queries are Datalog, for the neurolang-query REPL (neurolang-query --engine neurostore), the --engine option of its one-shot mode, or execute_datalog_program().

What is in the ontology#

The ontology is loaded with individuals: true and names: prefixed, so that a triple s p o is the fact p(s, o), with classes and properties named after their prefix, and the IRIs of the classes (http://www.cognitiveatlas.org/ontology/cogat.owl#CAO_00587) are their values. The ones to know:

rdfs:label(class, text)

the name of a concept, task or contrast, capitalised ('Working memory'); contrasts are named Task_name-contrast ('Digit_span_task-backward').

skos:definition(class, text)

its definition.

neurolang:subClassOf(class, super_class)

the class hierarchy. rdfs:subClassOf also holds the axioms that are not a class, such as the restrictions below.

owl:onProperty(restriction, property), owl:someValuesFrom(restriction, class)

the existential restrictions. A contrast that measures a concept is a subclass of the restriction measures some <concept>.

and, defined by the engine with rules over those:

cogat_subclass_of(class, ancestor)

the hierarchy at any depth: the closure of neurolang:subClassOf.

cogat_measures(contrast, concept)

the contrasts (and tasks) that measure a concept.

cogat_term(class, term)

the label in lower case, to join with the terms of mentions and term_in_study_tfidf.

They are listed with --list-predicates, and the sorts are cogat_class (an IRI) and cogat_term.

Looking up concepts#

A concept is found by its label, and what is known of it by its IRI:

ans(d) :- rdfs:label(c, 'Working memory'), skos:definition(c, d)

ans(parent) :- rdfs:label(c, 'Working memory'),
    neurolang:subClassOf(c, p), rdfs:label(p, parent)

The first returns the definition of working memory, “active maintenance and flexible updating of goal-task relevant information…”, the second its parent, Cognitive control.

Below a concept#

The classes below Cognitive control, at any depth (16 of them, among which Attention, Auditory working memory, Divided attention and Listening):

ans(l) :- rdfs:label(c, 'Cognitive control'),
    cogat_subclass_of(x, c), rdfs:label(x, l)

and the contrasts that measure working memory, e.g. Digit_span_task-backward:

ans(l) :- rdfs:label(c, 'Working memory'),
    cogat_measures(x, c), rdfs:label(x, l)

Any other relation of the ontology is read the same way, through its restrictions. For instance cogat_measures is defined as:

cogat_measures(x, c) :- rdfs:subClassOf(x, r),
    owl:onProperty(r, <http://www.cognitiveatlas.org/ontology/cogat.owl#measures>),
    owl:someValuesFrom(r, c)

and the properties used by the restrictions of the ontology (descended_from, has_contrast, has_part, is_related_to, measured_by, measures, part_of) are listed by:

ans(p) :- owl:onProperty(r, p)

From the ontology to the studies#

The ontology becomes useful with the data: its labels are the terms of the studies. The studies that mention working memory or any concept below it (301 studies below it in the 2026-10 NeuroStore release, among the 2,031 that mention working memory itself):

ans(s) :- rdfs:label(c, 'Working memory'),
    cogat_subclass_of(x, c), cogat_term(x, t), mentions(s, t)

ans(s) :- mentions(s, 'working memory')

and the voxels they report, to map the concept with all its sub-concepts:

ans(i, j, k, s) :- rdfs:label(c, 'Cognitive control'),
    cogat_subclass_of(x, c), cogat_term(x, t), mentions(s, t),
    reports(s, i, j, k)

The opposite join, which Cognitive Atlas labels the studies talk about and how often (282 of them in the 2026-10 release, the first being Activation, Memory and Attention):

ans(l, agg_count(s)) :- rdfs:label(x, l), cogat_term(x, t),
    mentions(s, t)

cogat_term lowercases the labels first. Writing the comparison in the query itself, (lcase(l) == t) between rdfs:label and mentions, gives the same answer but compares every label with every row of mentions, which is about ten times slower.

mentions keeps the TF-IDF above 0.03. To weigh the studies instead, use weighted_mention(s, t, w) or term_in_study_tfidf(s, t, w) in the same rule. In the neurostore engine the terms are words and sequences of up to three words of the abstracts, so multi-word concepts such as working memory or spatial working memory are terms; the neurosynth vocabulary has its own multi-word terms.

Using the ontology without an engine#

The same queries run on any frontend that loaded the file:

from neurolang.frontend import NeurolangPDL

nl = NeurolangPDL()
nl.load_ontology(
    "cogat.v2.owl", individuals=True, names="prefixed"
)
nl.execute_datalog_program("""
    cogat_subclass_of(x, y) :- neurolang:subClassOf(x, y)
    cogat_subclass_of(x, z) :-
        cogat_subclass_of(x, y), neurolang:subClassOf(y, z)
""")
below = nl.execute_datalog_program(
    "ans(l) :- rdfs:label(c, 'Working memory'),"
    " cogat_subclass_of(x, c), rdfs:label(x, l)"
)

To use the ontology in an engine of your own, add it to the ontologies of its entry in engines.yaml, or extend coordinate_meta_analysis (see Engine Configuration (engines.yaml)):

ontologies:
  - url: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CognitiveAtlas/cogat-ontology/master/ontology/cogat.v2.owl
    individuals: true
    names: prefixed
